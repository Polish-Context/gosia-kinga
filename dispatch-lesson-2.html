Marta usually keeps her weekends quiet. At the weekend, she rarely checks her email, and this time she made a point of staying completely offline during the weekend before a demanding new project began on Monday.

When she gets back to the office, her colleague Tomasz asks how she managed to sound so confident on yesterday's call. “I presume you've been practising,” he says. Marta admits that she tries to be consistent: she makes time to revise vocabulary for ten minutes every morning before she even opens her inbox, whatever the schedule looks like that day.

“You clearly have more discipline than me,” Tomasz replies. “Only because I make it a habit,” Marta says. “Otherwise the two of us would still be struggling with the same phrases from January.” Tomasz laughs and thanks her for the tip. “You're welcome,” she says, “although don't expect me to correct your reports for free. Just kidding, of course.”

At the beginning of the week, Marta's manager asks her to prepare a short training case study about handling overdue accounts. The names in the example should be invented so that no real client is identifiable: the company and the figures should be entirely fictitious.

Marta builds the case study around a fictional client that has fallen behind on its invoices. She explains how the finance team tracks payment arrears, when the first reminder is sent, and what happens if the balance is still outstanding at the end of the month.

Once the document is finished, she adds a table of contents so new starters can find the relevant section quickly. Then she drives to the regional office to present it in person, taking the motorway rather than the slower route through town.
