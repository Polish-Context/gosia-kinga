Marta has been cooperating with the Lisbon team since April, but the two teams have never seen each other in person. For that reason she organises an introductory meeting for Tuesday morning, so that her own team can get to know the Lisbon colleagues better.

Tomasz has been invited late, so he can only join the meeting after the first ten minutes. “Sorry I'm late,” he says quietly.

Marta gives a short presentation showing which countries the company is active in. As time is running out, she closes the discussion, and the two teams agree that the new reporting tool will go live on the nineteenth of October.

Afterwards, Tomasz suggests a photo session for the project page on the intranet, and Marta promises to plan some after-work activities for the Lisbon team's visit to Warsaw in November.

Tomasz has to present the project plan to the Lisbon team, and he asks Marta for advice on how to start. “You could open with questions,” she says. “Or you could begin by telling the audience about a recent conversation with a client. You could also start with a personal story.”

During the presentation, Tomasz notices that the audience is hesitating to interrupt him. “Don't be shy,” he says. “You can ask me questions after I finish the presentation.” Just then, someone in the audience sneezes, and Tomasz says, “Bless you!” Everyone laughs, and the atmosphere becomes more relaxed.

Later that day, Marta's manager asks her when the final slides will be ready. Marta has not been told about any deadline, and she nearly answers, “How would I know that?” She stops herself, because the question can sound sharp when it is addressed to a manager, and says instead that she has not received the deadline yet.

The manager replies that she needs the slides by close of business on Friday. Tomasz promises to send his part by the end of play on Friday and to prepare the tables as they discussed on Monday.
